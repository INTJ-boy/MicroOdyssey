The discovery timeline

Seven eras of microbiology, from the first microscope to synthetic biology. Every entry links to a scientist, discovery, or species already documented in this archive.

Microscopic era 1670s - 1850s

1676
Antonie van Leeuwenhoek observes animalcules through a hand-ground lens in Delft, the first documented sighting of microbial life.
1683 - 1700
Leeuwenhoek continues cataloguing microscopic life, including protozoa such as Giardia and Volvox globator.

Microbiology emerges 1840s - 1870s

1847
Ignaz Semmelweis shows hand-washing with chlorinated lime sharply reduces childbed fever in Vienna, before germ theory existed to explain it.
1854
Filippo Pacini first describes the comma-shaped bacterium found in cholera victims.
1861
Louis Pasteur's swan-neck flask experiments disprove spontaneous generation.
1867
Joseph Lister applies germ theory to surgery, introducing carbolic acid antisepsis.
1873
Gerhard Armauer Hansen identifies the leprosy bacillus, the first bacterium ever linked to a human disease.

Golden age of microbiology 1876 - 1910

1876
Robert Koch proves Bacillus anthracis causes anthrax, the first bacterium ever proven to cause a specific disease.
1880 - 1884
Koch's postulates are formulated; Staphylococcus aureus, Streptococcus pneumoniae, and Mycobacterium tuberculosis are identified.
1884
Hans Christian Gram develops the differential staining method still used to classify bacteria today.
1892 - 1898
Ivanovsky and Beijerinck discover viruses as agents distinct from bacteria, using the tobacco mosaic disease.
1894
Alexandre Yersin isolates the plague bacillus in Hong Kong during the Third Plague Pandemic.
1905
Schaudinn and Hoffmann identify the spirochete that causes syphilis.

Antibiotic era 1917 - 1950s

1917
Felix d'Herelle discovers bacteriophages, coining the term after identifying viruses that kill bacteria.
1928
Alexander Fleming discovers penicillin after mold contamination kills bacteria on a culture plate.
1943
Selman Waksman and Albert Schatz isolate streptomycin, the first effective tuberculosis treatment.

Molecular era 1928 - 1970s

1928
Frederick Griffith discovers bacterial transformation, hinting at a transferable genetic substance.
1944
Avery, MacLeod, and McCarty show DNA, not protein, carries genetic information.
1946 - 1952
Lederberg, Tatum, Zinder, Hershey, and Chase reveal conjugation, transduction, and confirm DNA as the genetic material.
1953
Watson, Crick, and Franklin reveal the double-helix structure of DNA.
1970
Restriction enzymes are discovered, enabling recombinant DNA technology.
1977
Carl Woese's rRNA sequencing reveals Archaea as a domain of life distinct from Bacteria.

Genomic era 1983 - 2000s

1983
Kary Mullis conceives PCR; Barre-Sinoussi and Montagnier isolate HIV.
1995 - 1996
The first bacterial genome (Haemophilus influenzae) and first archaeal genome (Methanocaldococcus jannaschii) are fully sequenced.
2007
The Human Microbiome Project launches, mapping microbial communities across the human body.

Modern microbiology 2012 - present

2012
Doudna and Charpentier adapt bacterial CRISPR into a programmable gene-editing tool.
2020
SARS-CoV-2 is identified and sequenced within weeks of the first reported cases in Wuhan.
